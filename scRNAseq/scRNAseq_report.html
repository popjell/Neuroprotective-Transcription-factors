Neuronal MS analysis: Schirmer and Trobisch

Schirmer and Trobisch were compared at donor level within neuronal subtypes. Trobisch is analyzed as one dataset across cortex, cerebellum, and spinal cord; region-by-region results show where a combined signal arises. Its full atlas includes 20 neuronal labels.

Summary

In Trobisch's combined, region-adjusted analysis, 7 of 29 testable subtype/list comparisons pass FDR with preranked GSEA, and 21 of 29 pass with ROAST. Every significant comparison is MS-up. However, most testable groups are cortex-only; a combined-dataset result does not mean all three regions support it.

In the region-by-region check, cerebellar GRAN-EN Rank 2 is nominally MS-up by ROAST (p 0.0238), but does not pass correction among cerebellar tests (q 0.0714). Spinal-cord donor-subtype samples are too sparse for these tests. Schirmer broad Rank 1 remains nonsignificant (q 0.155).

Trobisch's finer cortical labels give a separate view of the reused Schirmer donors. The broad UCell scores are MS-up: Rank 1 p 0.00129 (q 0.00129); Rank 2 p 0.000152 (q 0.000304). This is supportive within the cortical cohort, not independent replication.

The broad cortical scores remain FDR-significant after separate adjustments for age, RNA integrity, post-mortem interval, median UMIs, detected genes, or mitochondrial percentage. 27 of 27 FDR-significant cortical subtype/list scores retain their MS-up direction after removing any one donor. With only eight donors, these checks cannot remove unmeasured batch or tissue differences.

The rank lists are not behaving as uniform 31-gene programs. Removing PSAP and ITM2C together removes the broad Rank 2 difference (p 0.305). Rank 1 remains higher after removing PSAP and PREX1 (p 0.00455), but its effect is much smaller.

A competitive gene-set test also finds MS-up enrichment in 20 cortical subtype/list comparisons after correction across all testable Trobisch comparisons. Only a few rank-list genes pass the expression filter in each subtype, so this supports a focused gene module rather than every gene on the lists.

Schirmer IN-VIP Rank 1 was higher in MS at p 0.00426 (q 0.0768) in the donor-level mean-expression test. It is a lead, not a confirmed signature. Rank-list genes passing global gene-level FDR were ABCC3, PREX1 in Schirmer, none in Trobisch's new regions, and CNDP2, ITM2C, PSAP, TMBIM6 in Trobisch cortex. Direction matters: an MS-down gene is not evidence that the rank list is up.

Single-sample Rank 1/2 scores

Seven scores were calculated from the same normalized donor-by-subtype pseudobulk profiles: ssGSEA, GSVA, singscore, AUCell, PLAGE, z-score, and gene-wise 0–1 min-max averaging. Each score was tested for MS versus control within every eligible neuronal label. Trobisch used cortex, cerebellum, and spinal cord together, adjusting the disease effect for region when a label had usable donors in multiple regions. The table counts q < 0.05 within each method; the two numbers are MS-up / MS-down. Methods reuse the same donors and genes, so agreement is a robustness check, not independent replication.

Interpretation

Combined Trobisch GSEA, ROAST, and single-sample scores find MS-up rank-list signals, but most testable observations are cortical. The cerebellar GRAN-EN Rank 2 score signal depends on scoring method and only six donors; spinal cord is too sparse. The cortical cells overlap Schirmer and show higher detected genes and UMIs in MS, so this is a focused cortical lead, not independent validation or a verified pan-neuronal human-MS signature.

Methods: raw counts were summed by donor and subtype; gene-level comparisons used DESeq2 for Schirmer and edgeR quasi-likelihood for Trobisch. Trobisch's primary gene-set test combines all usable donors with region in the model; region-specific results are sensitivity checks within that dataset. Rank-list scores used normalized pseudobulk expression and donor-level tests; UCell and camera were supporting checks. Sources: Schirmer et al. 2019 and Trobisch et al. 2022.
